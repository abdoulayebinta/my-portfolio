## Observation

When building a product for multiple user types, the path to adoption is not always the path to engagement. At Boukili, a French-language reading platform for children 4–12 designed for both classroom and home use, we discovered this the hard way.

## What I Noticed

During early adoption analysis, account creation data showed a clear pattern: parents accounted for 65% of all account signups. The immediate reading was obvious—optimize for parents. They were the bottleneck to growth.

But when we examined behavior beyond signup, the picture became more complicated.

## Why It Matters

In edtech and platform businesses, the person who creates the account is often not the person who generates value for the product. Parents might sign up their children; teachers might adopt classroom by classroom. But whose sustained engagement actually determines whether the product survives?

Confusing acquisition with usage can misdirect an entire product roadmap.

## What It Changed in My Thinking

This experience taught me to separate two questions:

1. **What is the path to adoption?** (How do users discover and try the product?)
2. **What is the core user?** (Who creates sustained, predictable engagement?)

Parents might be the adoption gate. Teachers might be the engagement engine.

Understanding both, and the difference between them, is essential to product strategy.

## Where I Would Apply This Again

This lesson applies broadly to any product with multiple stakeholder types:

- B2B SaaS where procurement differs from usage
- Platforms serving families or groups (kids' apps, family tools, schools)
- Products with institutional and individual users
- Any two-sided marketplace where the referrer differs from the user

The framework is the same: validate engagement patterns before optimizing for adoption channels.
